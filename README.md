# Real-World-Retail-Data-Processing-and-Quality-Control
#Retail data processing, cleaning, validation and quality control using Microsoft Excel#




For this project, I worked with 541,909 sales transactions from a UK-based online gift retailer. Using Microsoft Excel, I examined the original dataset, identified data quality problems, and worked on preparing the records for reliable business reporting and analysis.
During the initial assessment, I found several issues, including missing customer information, duplicate transactions, inconsistent product descriptions, invalid prices, unusual quantities, and inconsistent country names.
I cleaned and standardised the data using clearly defined rules. Rather than removing every record that appeared unusual, I assessed the issues individually and decided whether each record should be retained, corrected, flagged for further review or excluded. I documented these decisions and maintained the original row numbers so that records could be traced back to the source dataset.
I organised the work into an Excel workbook with six worksheets: Dashboard, Data_Profiling, Cleaning_Log, Exception_Report, Cleaned_Data and Quality_Report. Together, they provide a clear record of the problems identified, the actions taken, and the final results.
I also developed 15 formula-driven quality control checks, all of which passed. This helped me verify that the cleaned dataset met the rules established during processing.
By the end of the exercise, I had retained 534,129 records (98.56% of the original dataset), excluded 7,780 records (1.44%) with documented reasons, and identified 12,240 retained records requiring further review.
The project gave me practical experience in handling large datasets, investigating data quality issues, applying cleaning rules, managing exceptions, and presenting results through Excel reports and dashboards. It also demonstrates the importance of checking data carefully and keeping a clear record of changes before using it for business analysis and decision-making.
